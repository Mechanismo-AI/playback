Mechanismo: playback

Production lines and single mechanisms designed by the Mechanismo generator, played in the browser from the same data as their FreeCAD models.

How they were made

Every mechanism here was designed by code from a written brief: where a part must be carried, how far, how still it must be held and for how long, how much space there is, and how accurately it will be built. The brief goes to a library of nine mechanism families. Each family has its own design search and its own independent check, written a different way from the search. Only designs that pass their check, fit the space, and still meet the brief on at least 90% of simulated copies built with the stated errors are kept, and the smallest of those is picked. A line is then checked as a whole: its timing, every hand-over, collisions through the full cycle, and 100 mis-built copies of the entire line.

No AI model is asked to make a mechanism. The designs come from geometry and calculation, not from a model's guess. That makes them:

  • Checkable. Every design comes with the measurements its checks made, and every page here is checked against its FreeCAD model.
  • Repeatable. The same brief and the same version of the code give the same design. A line regenerated from its brief came out the same, byte for byte.
  • Honest about failure. A brief that no design meets gets no design, and the reason is recorded.

Lines

    Three stations on one 2 s cycle carry a 25 mm puck between two fixed nests: a plate cam brings it in, a barrel cam carries it past a label head, and a slider-crank takes it out.

  • Test line B: an index-table line
    A plate cam brings each part in, a Geneva wheel indexes the table, and a slider-crank takes the part out.
  • The puck line, nests 140 mm apart
    The puck line regenerated with its nests 20 mm farther apart.
  • The puck line, for a 30 mm puck
    The puck line regenerated for a larger puck.
  • The everything line: a stress test, not a working line
    Nine stations on one 1.5 s cycle, each brief written to draw a different family, so that one line uses all nine. It was put together to run every family through one line, and the checks that judge a line as a whole were built and tested on it; it was left as first laid out. Those checks find that it cannot be built: ten pairs of stations collide; five of its eight hand-overs fail (three are open for under 5 ms of the 50 needed, and two never open); and none of 100 mis-built copies works. What it taught: a part must be held still where it changes hands, stations need the room their own mechanisms take, and choosing each station on its own does not make a line. Every other line here was designed after those checks existed, and passes them.

The single designs below were made before the transmission rule of 19 November 2026, which asks a linkage to keep its transmission angle at 30 degrees or more through its whole cycle, and a rod that drives a slide at 45 or more. Seven of them fall short of it: all four first designs, and the four-bar, geared five-bar and dwell linkage. Each of those pages says so, with what its design measures; today's selector would not pick them.

The project's first designs, each for a brief of its own:

  • The dwell station: a dwell linkage
    Holds a carried point still within 0.3 mm at a labelling station for 50 degrees of the cycle, while the machine keeps turning.
  • The gap transfer: a slider-crank
    Lifts a part off one belt, carries it level across a 260 mm gap and sets it down on the next.

Each page shows only what its model's data holds: from the front, the back, either end, above or below, or from above at a corner, with no shading or perspective. The corner views show what is in front, as the model is built, and leave out the base boards so that the mechanisms show. It is for looking: the FreeCAD model stays the model for building.
